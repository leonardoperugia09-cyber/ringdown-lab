# Start time and loudness decide overtone detection

Whether you can detect the first overtone of a black-hole ringdown (the 221 mode) depends mostly on two things. The first is **how much signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) is left at the moment your fit starts**. The second is **how early you dare to start that fit**. The overtone has almost the same pitch as the fundamental 220 mode, but it fades about three times faster: roughly 1.3–1.4 ms versus 4 ms for a GW150914-like remnant. So it exists only in the first few milliseconds after the peak. That is exactly where the peak time is uncertain and where a simple sum of damped sinusoids is least trustworthy. Optimistic Fisher-matrix studies say a ringdown SNR of about **8–15** is enough to show the overtone's amplitude is non-zero, and about **30** is needed to resolve its frequency. Realistic Bayesian analyses may need SNR near **100** for precision tests ([Bhagwat et al. 2020, arXiv:1910.08708](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08708); [Jiménez Forteza et al. 2020, arXiv:2005.03260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03260)). The real-data record fits that picture. GW150914 had a post-peak SNR of about 14, and its overtone claim (3.6σ) is still disputed: analyses that account for the uncertain peak time find only about 2σ. GW250114 had about 40 post-merger. There the overtone amplitude is non-zero at **4.1σ** when the fit starts 6 t_Mf (≈2.0 ms) after the peak, and stays above 3σ until 9 t_Mf (≈3.0 ms) ([LVK, arXiv:2509.08054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08054)). The physics community has no single agreed definition of "detected", and the published numbers depend on which test was used. For Ringdown Lab, the practical lesson is this: a white-noise, two-damped-sinusoid model is a fair teaching tool, but it must use the detector-frame mass (~68.1–68.4 M☉, not 62.7 M☉). It must also state at what time the 221/220 amplitude ratio is defined, and it should show detection significance as a function of start time, not as a single yes/no answer.

## Three different questions hide behind "detected"

The literature uses at least three detection tests. They can disagree on the same data, and that disagreement is the core of the GW150914 controversy.

The first test asks **whether the overtone amplitude is clearly not zero**. Isi et al. found that for GW150914 "the mean of the A1 marginalized posterior lies 3.6 standard deviations away from zero" when the fit started at the strain peak ([Isi et al. 2019, arXiv:1905.00869](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00869)). That "σ" is just (posterior mean)/(posterior standard deviation). It is not a false-alarm probability. The LVK's GW250114 companion paper defines significance more carefully, as the Gaussian-equivalent level of the smallest credible interval that excludes zero (so 3σ means 99.73%). With an **agnostic two-damped-sinusoid fit**, which is the same kind of model Ringdown Lab uses, the faster-decaying sinusoid stays >3σ from zero until 9 t_Mf and is at 3.5σ at 6 t_Mf ([LVK, arXiv:2509.08099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08099)). Isi & Farr add a sobering point. Repeating a 3.5σ-on-average injection in fresh noise gives results scattered by about ±1σ, so the 3.6σ GW150914 result "could just as easily have been a marginal 2σ" or a 5σ result ([Isi & Farr 2022, arXiv:2202.02941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02941)).

The second test asks **whether adding the overtone improves the fit by more than the cost of the extra parameters**. The tool is the Bayes factor B between a 220+221 model and a 220-only model. On the Kass–Raftery scale used in ringdown work, B > 3.2 is "substantial", B > 10 "strong" and B > 100 "decisive" ([Cabero et al. 2020, arXiv:1911.01361](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01361)). Cotesta et al. find log10 B = −0.60 for GW150914 at the start time Isi et al. used. Decisive evidence (log10 B > 2) appears only for start times ≲ −4.34M, that is, before the merger peak, "well outside of the nominal region of validity" ([Cotesta et al. 2022, arXiv:2201.00822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00822)). Isi & Farr argue that Bayes factors are "an especially poor tool" here, because widening the prior on A1 shifts the whole curve ([arXiv:2202.02941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02941)). Cotesta et al. reply that they narrowed their prior as much as possible without cutting off the posterior, so the objection does not apply to their numbers ([arXiv:2201.00822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00822)). Bayes factors also depend on the analysis code even for GW250114. At 6 t_Mf, pyRing gives log10 B = 0.56 ± 0.26 while the `ringdown` code gives 2.51 ([arXiv:2509.08099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08099)).

The third test asks **whether noise alone could produce a fit this good**. These are frequentist tests. Berti et al. 2007 derived a generalized likelihood-ratio test (GLRT) critical SNR for deciding that a second mode is present. They concluded that, once a ringdown has been detected, "the most important criterion … seems to be the GLRT criterion". Their worked numbers were for angular modes, not overtones, and the authors call the estimate optimistic ([Berti et al. 2007, arXiv:0707.1202](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.1202)). For GW250114, a QNM rational-filter statistic compared against its 1% false-alarm threshold "finds strong support for the 220+221 model" for start times ≲ 10 t_Mf ([arXiv:2509.08099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08099)).

### Distinguishability sets the floor, and resolvability sets a higher bar

A fourth idea, from Lindblom, Owen & Brown, sets a floor beneath all of these tests. Two waveforms differing by δh are **indistinguishable when ⟨δh|δh⟩ < 1**. Here ⟨·|·⟩ is the noise-weighted inner product, so ||δh|| is literally "the SNR of the difference". The authors show that, to lowest order, only the part of δh orthogonal to the true waveform counts, because the parallel part merely rescales the amplitude ([Lindblom, Owen & Brown 2008, arXiv:0809.3844](https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.3844)). Giesler et al. applied the equivalent mismatch form, **M < 1/(2·SNR²)**, to ringdown models. They found that at post-peak SNR 42.3, templates with mismatch ≲ 3×10⁻⁴ are "effectively identical", so differences between models with 3 or more overtones cannot be measured ([Giesler et al. 2019, arXiv:1903.08284](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08284)).

Applied to the 221 mode, the criterion gives a practical number: the **SNR carried by the overtone alone**, ρ₂₂₁ = ||h₂₂₁||. If ρ₂₂₁ < 1, the 220-only and 220+221 models cannot be told apart. This is our own synthesis; no paper we found states it under that name. It comes with two important qualifications. Because 221 and 220 have nearly the same frequency, part of h₂₂₁ can be absorbed by shifting the 220's amplitude, phase, mass and spin, so only the orthogonal leftover counts. Also, ||δh⊥|| > 1 is necessary for detection but nowhere near enough for a 3σ claim.

**Resolvability** is a separate, stricter bar. It asks whether the overtone's frequency or damping time can be measured as different from the fundamental's. It follows a Rayleigh-type rule: |f₁ − f₂| > max(σ_f₁, σ_f₂) ([Berti, Cardoso & Will 2006, arXiv:gr-qc/0512160](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0512160)).

## Overtones turn the start time into a bias-versus-noise trade-off

When the fit starts matters as much as the SNR. A noise-free numerical-relativity (NR) waveform makes this clear. Fitting only the 220 mode from the strain peak "provides largely biased estimates" of remnant mass and spin, because the overtones dominate the waveform there. The bias disappears only if the fit waits until about **47M after the peak** ([Giesler et al. 2019, arXiv:1903.08284](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08284)). In simulated noise at post-peak SNR 42.3, the 220-only fit encloses the true mass and spin at 90% only for starts ≳ **5 ms** after the peak, and these later fits are much less precise. An N = 3 overtone fit from the peak is both accurate and tighter ([arXiv:1903.08284](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08284)).

Bhagwat et al. turned this into a recipe. **Bias depends only on the model and the start time; statistical error shrinks as 1/SNR.** The optimal start time is where the two are equal ([Bhagwat et al. 2020, arXiv:1910.08708](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08708)). A one-tone fit from the peak biases the fundamental frequency by **15%** for a GW150914-like system (SXS:0305) and 12% for a mass-ratio-4 system (SXS:1220). Adding the 221 lets a GW150914-loud signal start at about **4M**. At **5× that SNR, the optimal start moves later, to about 14M**, because the same fixed bias now stands out against smaller error bars ([arXiv:1910.08708](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08708)). Giesler et al. make the same point. The original LIGO GW150914 test found 220-only agreement at 3 ms probably because "at lower SNRs, the statistical errors outweigh the systematic errors" ([arXiv:1903.08284](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08284)). Louder signals do not remove the need to model overtones; they make it more urgent.

### The GW150914 dispute is about milliseconds of timing

**Contested.** For GW150914, every analysis agrees that the overtone evidence changes by orders of magnitude within a few M of the peak. Correia et al. note that both Cotesta et al. and Isi & Farr saw the Bayes factor rise "by several orders of magnitude in less than 5M" (≈1.7 ms). Meanwhile the merger-time uncertainty is ≈1.2 ms ([Correia et al. 2023, arXiv:2312.14118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14118)). The overtone's whole lifetime is about 1.3 ms ≈ 4M, yet Cotesta et al. found the evidence jumping over shifts of only 0.72M ([arXiv:2201.00822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.00822)).

Isi & Farr's 2023 Comment reported a timestamp "coding bug", a different start time and a data segment that was too short in that analysis. After fixing these, they find that significance "does not ultimately drop below 2σ until ~10 ms after the reference time" ([Isi & Farr 2023, arXiv:2310.13869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13869)). Carullo et al. reply that the bug shifts times by only about 0.06 ms, about 40× smaller than the ~2.5 ms peak-time uncertainty. Once that uncertainty is folded in, they find about **1.8σ** ([Carullo et al. 2023, arXiv:2310.20625](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20625)).

Independent analyses that marginalise over the start time land low. Finch & Moore report ~1.8σ ([arXiv:2205.07809](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07809)), and Correia et al. a Bayes factor of **1.10 ± 0.03** ([arXiv:2312.14118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14118)). A calibrated simulation-based inference study reports about 2σ, but we saw only a summary of that paper, not its full text ([Crisostomi et al. 2023, arXiv:2305.18528](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18528)). The cautious reading is "tentative". Isi & Farr do not accept that reading.

### Many-overtone fits may absorb physics that is not ringing

**Also contested.** It is not settled what extra overtones near the peak physically represent. Baibhav et al. show that a seven-overtone fit's tiny mismatch can be pushed down indefinitely by adding parameters. They argue overtones with n ≥ 2 mainly "fit away" initial-data effects, tails and nonlinearities. Only 220, 221 and 320 are robustly identifiable in the ℓ = m = 2 multipole. Even with no noise, there is convincing evidence for the 221 "only well after the peak" ([Baibhav et al. 2023, arXiv:2302.03050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03050)). Quadratic (nonlinear) modes have since been found in NR ringdowns, especially in the (4,4) harmonic ([Mitman et al., arXiv:2208.07380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07380); [Cheung et al., arXiv:2208.07374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07374)). This means a pure linear sum of modes is incomplete close to the peak. The LVK GW250114 fits do not model nonlinear modes, tails or the prompt response ([arXiv:2509.08099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08099)).

## GW250114 heard the overtone at SNR 26 from 2 ms after the peak

GW250114 is the clearest overtone measurement so far, because it is so loud. The network matched-filter SNR was **80** in the PRL abstract and 77–80 across search pipelines (Hanford 53, Livingston 60). It was observed by the two LIGO detectors only ([LVK, arXiv:2509.08054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08054)). The companion paper splits the signal into about **65 before merger and ~40 after** ([arXiv:2509.08099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08099)). The SNR that actually matters for the overtone is smaller. The two-mode ringdown recovered **SNR 26 starting at 6 t_Mf**, which by itself equals GW150914's entire SNR. The single-mode fit at 10.5 t_Mf recovered **SNR 21** ([arXiv:2509.08054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08054)).

The PRL found the 221 amplitude "nonzero at 4.1σ credibility for t0 = 6 t_Mf", above 3σ until 9.0 t_Mf, and below 1σ after 10.5 t_Mf. From 10.5 t_Mf onward, one mode is enough ([arXiv:2509.08054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08054)). Here t_Mf = 0.337 ms, so the overtone is visible for roughly **2.0 to 3.0 ms after the peak**. Before 6 t_Mf the fitted amplitudes stop decaying like clean exponentials, which the LVK says "hints at unmodeled features". Measured detector-frame values are:

| Mode | Fit start | Frequency | Damping |
|---|---|---|---|
| 221 | 6 t_Mf | f₂₂₁ = 249 (+8/−9) Hz | γ₂₂₁ = 708 (+116/−107) s⁻¹ |
| 220 | 10.5 t_Mf | f₂₂₀ = 247 ± 6 Hz | γ₂₂₀ = 221 (+39/−32) s⁻¹ |

The 221 damping rates correspond to τ ≈ 1.4 ms (our conversion, τ = 1/γ). The 221 frequency is consistent with Kerr to ±30% (δf₂₂₁ = 0.1 ± 0.3), but **its damping time is unconstrained** within the prior ([arXiv:2509.08054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08054)). The measured amplitudes and phases agree with an NR simulation at the ≈38% credible level ([arXiv:2509.08099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08099)).

**Frame matters.** The remnant mass **M_f = 62.7 (+1.0/−1.1) M☉ is the source-frame value**. The detector-frame (redshifted) mass, which sets the frequencies LIGO actually records, is **(1+z)M_f = 68.1 (+0.8/−0.9) M☉** at z ≈ 0.09. The ringdown analyses use a reference value of 68.409 M☉ ([arXiv:2509.08054, Tables I–II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08054)). Our own estimate from the Berti–Cardoso–Will fits shows the difference. With 62.7 M☉ and χ = 0.68 you get f₂₂₀ ≈ 271 Hz and τ₂₂₀ ≈ 3.8 ms, which is 8–9% off what was measured. With 68.4 M☉ you get f₂₂₀ ≈ 249 Hz, τ₂₂₀ ≈ 4.1 ms, f₂₂₁ ≈ 243 Hz and τ₂₂₁ ≈ 1.35 ms, matching the data.

Independent reanalyses broadly support the overtone. An orthonormal-mode analysis raises support for the first overtone "from 82.5% to 99.9%" ([Suzuki et al. 2026, arXiv:2605.03576](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03576)). One group claims quadratic modes at 3.4σ, but that paper was substantially revised and has not been published in a journal ([Wang et al. 2026, arXiv:2601.05734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05734)). We found no published critique of the GW250114 overtone. We did not read these reanalyses in full, so treat their details as unverified.

## The noise floor is flat for the fundamental but not quite for the overtone

LIGO's sensitivity forms a "bucket". Below about 50 Hz, control, seismic and radiation-pressure noise dominate. Across roughly **50–500 Hz, mirror coating thermal noise dominates, with quantum noise second**. Above about 450 Hz, shot noise takes over ([Capote et al. 2025, arXiv:2411.14607](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14607)). The coating noise follows a smooth, gentle power law (∝ f^−0.45 in displacement), so the floor is nearly flat across the middle of the band. On the official O4 projection curves, the strain noise amplitude (ASD) changes by only about **14% between 150 and 500 Hz** and up to ~29% between 100 and 500 Hz. These ratios are our arithmetic on the tabulated curves. The minimum is about **3.0×10⁻²⁴ /√Hz near 329 Hz** on the optimistic "O4 high" curve and **3.45×10⁻²⁴ near 269 Hz** on the "O4 low" curve ([LIGO-T2000012-v2 O4high](https://dcc.ligo.org/public/0165/T2000012/002/aligo_O4high.txt); [O4low](https://dcc.ligo.org/public/0165/T2000012/002/aligo_O4low.txt)). The O4 low curve is closer to the median ranges achieved in O4, 152–160 Mpc ([Capote et al. 2025](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/176909/)). Below 50 Hz the noise rises like a wall, to about 14× the minimum ASD at 20 Hz.

The overtone's short life is what limits the white-noise picture. A damped sinusoid has a Lorentzian spectrum with half-width 1/(2πτ) ([Berti, Cardoso & Will 2006, arXiv:gr-qc/0512160](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0512160)). For the 220 (τ ≈ 4 ms), the full width is about 80 Hz, spanning roughly 210–290 Hz, where the ASD varies by only ~2%. White noise is essentially exact there. For the 221 (τ ≈ 1.3 ms), the full width is about 250–320 Hz and the quality factor is below 1: the mode dies in less than one cycle. Its slowly falling tails reach the steep low-frequency wall. A white-noise model therefore over-counts the SNR contributed by low frequencies. We estimate the error at tens of percent; this is our own order-of-magnitude estimate, not a published result. Berti, Cardoso & Will's standard ringdown SNR formula itself assumes the noise is constant across the mode and is valid only for "large quality factor" ([gr-qc/0512160](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0512160)).

Real data add two more complications. The first is narrow lines: 60 Hz mains harmonics at 240 and 300 Hz, and beam-splitter violin modes near 300 Hz, all right in the ringdown band, plus glitches ([GWOSC O4 lines](https://gwosc.org/O4/o4speclines)). The second is that cutting the data at t₀ breaks the periodicity assumed by frequency-domain methods. Real ringdown codes therefore use a time-domain likelihood whose covariance matrix comes from the noise autocorrelation function. Isi & Farr warn that mishandling this is "more pronounced when targeting short-lived modes" such as the overtone ([Isi & Farr 2021, arXiv:2107.05609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.05609)). Whitening also mixes in data from a span about as long as the overtone's damping time, so results depend on data just before t₀ ([arXiv:2312.14118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14118)).

## Amplitude ratio and phase can swing the required SNR by 4×

The 221 is not a weak afterthought. In NR fits of a GW150914-like merger (SXS:BBH:0305), **A₂₂₁/A₂₂₀ ≈ 4.0 at the strain peak** (A₀ = 0.974, A₁ = 3.89), and the fundamental "does not dominate … until roughly 10M after t_peak" ([Giesler et al. 2019, arXiv:1903.08284](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08284)). Ota & Chirenti quote **R ≈ 0.66 measured at t_peak + 10M** for equal-mass non-spinning binaries. For mass ratios up to about 5:1, the 221 is larger than any higher angular mode ([Ota & Chirenti 2020, arXiv:1911.00440](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00440)). These two numbers agree once you account for the overtone's faster decay. The ratio falls as exp(−0.177 t/M), which takes 4 down to ≈0.68 at 10M (our arithmetic using Bhagwat et al.'s τ values). **A ratio without a reference time is meaningless.** The fitted ratio also drifts with the fit start time: a two-tone model gives A₁ ≈ 1 at t₀ = 0 but ≈4 at t₀ = 25M ([Bhagwat et al. 2020, arXiv:1910.08708](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08708)).

Relative phase matters just as much. An in-phase overtone (φ ≈ 0) "increases the SNR by a few times", while a counter-phase one "decreases the overall SNR significantly". For resolving the overtone frequency, however, φ = π needs an SNR **4–5× smaller** than φ = 0. Across combinations of ratio and phase, the minimum SNR for resolvability ranges from **≈28 at best to ≈100** over much of parameter space ([arXiv:1910.08708](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08708)). These two facts are not contradictory, because they describe different quantities. Total SNR measures how loud the sum is. Resolvability measures how different the two tones look. Our interpretation, not the authors', is that destructive interference near the peak makes the change in decay rate more visible.

Because the frequencies differ by only about **2% (≈5 Hz)** while the damping times differ by **3×**, the overtone shows up mainly as **extra, fast-fading amplitude right after the peak**, not as a second pitch. Fisher studies find its quality factor resolvable at ρ ≲ 10, compared with ρ ≳ 30 for its frequency ([Jiménez Forteza et al. 2020, arXiv:2005.03260](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.03260)). Real data show the opposite. In both GW150914 and GW250114 the frequency was constrained while the damping time was not ([arXiv:1905.00869](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00869); [arXiv:2509.08054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08054)). Damping time trades off against amplitude and start time in ways a Fisher estimate does not capture.

## What this means for Ringdown Lab

A white-noise, two-damped-sinusoid model is scientifically defensible for teaching. It is close to the LVK's own agnostic "2DS" fit for GW250114, and LIGO's noise floor is genuinely near-flat where the 220 lives. Set the noise level from the ASD at about 250–300 Hz, around 3.5×10⁻²⁴ /√Hz for O4-like data. For discrete white noise sampled at f_s, the standard per-sample standard deviation is σ ≈ ASD·√(f_s/2). That formula is standard signal processing, not from the cited papers.

**Use detector-frame parameters**: (1+z)M_f ≈ 68.4 M☉, χ ≈ 0.68, f₂₂₀ ≈ 249 Hz, τ₂₂₀ ≈ 4.1 ms, f₂₂₁ ≈ 243 Hz, τ₂₂₁ ≈ 1.35 ms. Measure time in t_Mf = 0.337 ms. Label every amplitude ratio with its reference time, for example "A₂₂₁/A₂₂₀ = 4 at peak" or "0.66 at peak + 10M". Expose the relative phase as a control alongside the ratio, since it changes the required SNR by up to 4–5×.

The most instructive thing the app can show is a **scan over start time**. For each t₀, fit 220-only and 220+221 models and plot four things: the overtone amplitude's significance (mean/std), the overtone's own SNR ρ₂₂₁ = ||h₂₂₁|| (with the Lindblom threshold of 1 marked), the bias in the recovered 220 frequency, and the 220's statistical error. Students will then see three results reproduce. Significance collapses within a few milliseconds. The bias–error crossing moves later as SNR rises. At GW250114-like SNR (~26 at 2 ms), the overtone clears 3σ only in a narrow window.

The app should also be honest about three limits. White noise over-credits the broad overtone's low-frequency tail. Results from a white-noise model are therefore somewhat optimistic for the 221; a quick check is to rerun with an O4 curve as the noise PSD. A toy model has no peak-time uncertainty, which is the single factor that drove the GW150914 dispute. Finally, a perfect two-mode injection has no nonlinearities, so "fitting from the peak works" in the app does not mean it works in nature.

## Uncertain or contested items

| Item | Status |
|---|---|
| GW150914 overtone detection | **Contested.** Results range from 3.6σ at a fixed start to ~1.8–2σ (B ≈ 1.1) when the peak time is marginalised. Isi & Farr and Carullo/Cotesta et al. still disagree. |
| Physical meaning of n ≥ 2 overtones near the peak | **Contested.** Baibhav et al. argue they are overfitting artifacts; Giesler et al. treat them as physical. |
| GW250114 network SNR | The PRL says 80; the companion paper's HTML rendered "~76" ambiguously. |
| GW250114 221 significance | 4.1σ (PRL, Kerr 220+221 model) versus 3.5σ (companion, agnostic 2DS), both at 6 t_Mf. These are different models, not a conflict. Bayes factors at the same start time differ by about 2 in log10 between codes. |
| GW150914 earlier log10 B ≈ 0.6 | Cotesta et al. cite ~0.6, but our extraction of the GWTC-2 table read 0.15. Unresolved. |
| White-noise error for the overtone | Our estimate of "tens of percent" is unpublished arithmetic. Check it numerically against an O4 PSD. |
| Fisher SNR thresholds (8–15, 30, 100) | These are lower bounds. Fisher errors underestimated GW150914's measured Δf₀ by nearly 2× (18 vs 10.3 Hz) ([arXiv:1910.08708](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08708)). |
| GW250114 reanalyses (quadratic modes, RingCWB, ParSpec) | Read only as abstracts or snippets. The quadratic-mode claim is revised and has not been published in a journal. |
| O4 flatness ratios | Computed from smooth projection curves. Real O4 spectra are noisier, change over time and contain lines. |

## Conclusion

Overtone detection is not simply a matter of being loud enough. It is a joint condition on SNR, start time, the amplitude ratio and phase at that start time, and how well the peak is located. The overtone's whole lifetime (~1.3 ms) is about the same size as GW150914's peak-time uncertainty, and that coincidence, more than any disagreement about physics, kept that claim unresolved. GW250114 succeeded for a specific reason. It had enough SNR (26) to reach a 3σ significance starting 2 ms after the peak, a point late enough that the merger's messy physics no longer dominates. That is the regime in which a two-mode model is plausibly valid.

For Ringdown Lab, the most valuable result is not a single threshold. It is the curve of significance against start time, and the fact that a louder signal pushes the safe start time later. If the app lets a student slide t₀, the SNR, the amplitude ratio and the phase, and watch the 3σ window open and close, it will teach the real lesson of this literature. The app's white-noise world is a best case: no peak-time uncertainty, no low-frequency wall and no nonlinear modes. Any "detection" it shows near the peak should therefore be read as an upper bound on what real data would allow.
